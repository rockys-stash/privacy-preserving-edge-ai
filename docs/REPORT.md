# Report: the accuracy, privacy and cost of keeping sensor data on the device

All numbers come from the latest runs in `results/` (commit `325068e`, clean work tree, all 30
UCI HAR subjects). The full tables and figures are generated by `edgebench report` into
[generated/results.md](generated/results.md); this report interprets them. Nothing here was typed
in from memory; where a number is quoted, the table it comes from is named.

## 1. Question and set-up

Three architectures run the same human-activity pipeline on replayed smartphone inertial
streams: **centralized** (raw windows go to the cloud), **edge** (the device computes 165 features
and sends those) and **fully local** (the device classifies and sends only the label). Each has
five cumulative privacy levels (L0 full, L1 minimise, L2 coarsen, L3 perturb, L4 aggregate;
PLAN §2). For every one of the 15 cells we measure activity accuracy on people the models never
saw (subject-grouped 5-fold cross-validation × 3 seeds), **exposure** (how often an adversary at
the cloud who enrolled all 30 people re-identifies the wearer from one payload; chance is 1/30 =
0.033), bytes per window, operation counts, modelled latency and an energy proxy.

## 2. Results by research question

**RQ1: the frontier (E1).** Centralized L0 is the most accurate cell (0.954, 95% CI 0.934–0.971)
and among the most exposing (re-identification 0.574). The Pareto set on accuracy against exposure
is centralized/L0, edge/L0, local/L2 and local/L4, with "transmit nothing" also on it. Fully local
L2 keeps 0.937 accuracy (−0.017 against centralized L0) at re-identification 0.057, close to
chance, while sending 1 byte per window instead of 4,608. Hypothesis supported: neither extreme
alone is optimal, and the local cells give up little accuracy for a large drop in exposure.

**RQ2: are features less identifying than raw signals? (E1, E2).** Barely. Edge L0 features are
re-identified at 0.558 against 0.574 for raw windows, and minimising and coarsening them (L1, L2)
only brings this to 0.526 and 0.516. Gait and posture survive feature extraction. Only the
architecture that sends labels lowers exposure to near chance (local L0 0.060). E2 shows the
ordering is stable: Kendall's τ against the reference adversary is 0.83–0.96 across two attacker
families and enrolment shares from 10% to 70%; stronger adversaries with more enrolment data
re-identify more (centralized L0 rises from 0.416 to 0.624) but the ranking of cells barely moves.
Hypothesis supported.

**RQ3: the price of formal local privacy (E3).** For labels, randomised response behaves exactly as
theory predicts (measured accuracy within 0.003 of the analytic expectation at every ε) and is
cheap: accuracy 0.856 at ε = 4 and 0.935 from ε = 8. For the 165-dimensional edge features,
Laplace noise destroys utility: accuracy stays at the majority-class floor (0.187) up to ε = 16 and
only reaches 0.292 at ε = 64. This is the dimensionality problem the hypothesis named, but it is
starker than expected; at the configured ε, edge/L3 and edge/L4 sit *below* the majority-class
floor and are dominated by sending nothing. Per-window ε also composes over time (one payload
every 1.28 s), so none of these guarantees is meaningful over a session without a budget.

**RQ4: when is fully local cheapest? (E4).** On bandwidth and latency to a decision, always
(1 byte per window against 660 for features and 4,608 for raw). On the energy proxy, fully local is
cheapest for the phone profile on every link and every radio factor from 0.01× to 10×, and for the
microcontroller on BLE and cellular. The one exception is a microcontroller on Wi-Fi with radio
energy at 0.01× the assumed value, where sending raw data (2.30 µJ) undercuts on-device
classification (5.09 µJ). The conclusion depends on the radio constant only at that extreme. These
are modelled costs with stated constants, not hardware measurements (D7).

**RQ5: does streaming reproduce batch? (E5).** Yes for every deterministic cell: all six L0 and L2
cells streamed exactly the batch decisions for the 2,947 windows of the 9 official test people. L4
cells include L3's random noise, so a different draw is expected to give different decisions; their
accuracies differ by 0.008 (local), 0.058 (centralized) and 0.076 (edge, which is at chance either
way). The centralized gap is about two standard errors for a single draw of roughly 370 blocks
(an estimate, not a test); a multi-draw comparison is listed as future work. Aggregation costs
latency: median 3.85 s and p95 8.97 s at L4 against about 10 ms otherwise, because a decision waits
for its block of 8 windows to fill.

## 3. What a practitioner should take from this

1. Moving feature extraction to the edge cuts bandwidth by 7× but does **not** protect identity.
2. Sending labels is the step that protects identity, and with smoothing (local/L2) it costs under
   two points of accuracy on unseen people.
3. Local differential privacy is practical for labels and impractical, at any useful ε, for
   high-dimensional feature vectors sent per window.
4. Aggregation trades seconds of latency for little privacy gain beyond what labels already give.

## 4. Threats to validity

- **One dataset, 30 people, one device position.** Re-identification among 30 enrolled people is
  far easier than among thousands; exposure is a lower bound for this population, not a population
  risk. Results may not transfer to other sensors or placements.
- **Exposure is one adversary family.** E2 varies strength and enrolment, but a better attacker
  (for example, a deep sequence model) could raise every number. The ranking, not the level, is the
  robust finding.
- **Costs are modelled.** No physical device or radio was measured (D7). Operation counts and bytes
  are exact for this implementation; joules are proxies whose sensitivity is shown in E4.
- **Models are deliberately light** (random-kernel ridge, gradient-boosted trees, int8 logistic
  regression; D3). A deep model might change accuracy levels; the comparison is between data paths
  under the same model families.
- **Randomised cells use one noise draw in E5.**

## 5. Reproducibility

`make benchmark` reruns E1–E5 on all 30 subjects (about 45 minutes on 4 CPUs) and regenerates
`docs/generated/results.md` and `docs/figures/`. The first E1 run (marked dirty because the report
writer was uncommitted) was rerun from a clean checkout; every accuracy and exposure value came out
identical (D10). `make smoke` runs the pipeline on 8 people with tiny models in CI; it is never
reported.
